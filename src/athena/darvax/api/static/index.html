    1. What DarvaX is

    DarvaX is an experimental, advisory-only reading of Nicolas Darvas' box method, applied to ATHENA's own candle history. It is a satellite module (ADR-010) — deliberately separate from ATHENA's own analytical pipeline.

    • It never contributes to ATHENA's score, confidence, risk sizing, Decision object or TradePlan. Turning DarvaX off changes nothing else ATHENA does.
    • It writes to its own database, db/darvax.db, separate from ATHENA's db/athena.db. It reads ATHENA's candles read-only and writes nothing back to them.
    • It never places an order. Recording a holding, running a sweep, or scanning a symbol are all reads and local writes — nothing here talks to a broker.
    • Its output carries the badge EXPERIMENTAL · UNVALIDATED everywhere it appears, for the reason in section 11.

    2. The Darvas box

    Darvas watched for a stock to trade inside a tight, repeating range — a box — bounded by a ceiling (the high it kept failing to clear) and a floor (the low it kept holding above). His method has exactly one thing to say about a box: wait for the top or the bottom to give way, then act on which one did.

    DarvaX confirms a ceiling or floor once it has survived 3 bars unbeaten (box.confirmation_bars — the deck names no number, so this is the value used by the classical Darvas implementations it cites), and treats a bounce of 5% or more as a genuine swing pivot rather than noise (swing.threshold_pct, deck p.32). A retest counts if price comes back within 2% of the ceiling and still closes above it (breakout.retest_tolerance_pct) — the deck shows retests qualitatively, without naming a tolerance, so this number is DarvaX's own and is stated as such rather than attributed to Darvas.

    3. The four DAR-CARD rules

    Everything DarvaX classifies traces back to exactly four rules, quoted verbatim from Darvas' own card (deck p.67). DarvaX adds no fifth rule and no judgement beyond them.

    A Hold
    “A stock is in a rising trend when it is in its topmost box. As long as it remains there its price fluctuations should be ignored and the stock is a HOLD.”
    B Buy
    “If the price of the stock moves above the top of this topmost box the stock becomes a BUY. A 10 percent stop-loss should be set on the first breakout.”
    C Sell
    “Having formed a new higher box, if the price falls below the bottom into the shaded area of this box the stock is a SELL.”
    D No reason
    “There is no reason to HOLD or BUY a stock that is not in its topmost box.”

    Rule C is also the trailing stop the method actually uses: Darvas never took profit at a level, he rode the trend until a new, higher box formed and then exited if price fell out of it. This is why DarvaX shows no target price (section 5).

    4. What each action means

    A tier says what the price is doing; an action says what that implies for you specifically — the same rule-C state means “get out” if you hold the instrument and nothing at all if you don't. Nine actions, no fewer, because collapsing “get out” and “nothing to do” into one label would be advice about a position that may not exist.

    5. Reading a card

    Now
    Today's close.
    Buy above
    The entry trigger — the prior day's high (deck p.44, “Enter above the Previous Day High Price”). Only set alongside a stop-loss, so most WATCH rows have none yet.
    Ceiling / Floor
    The top and bottom of the topmost box.
    Stop-loss
    See section 8.
    Risk now
    What buying at today's close would risk if the stop-loss is hit — (close − stop) / close. Deliberately not risk-to-trigger: the stop is defined 10% below the trigger by construction, so that figure is a constant 10% on every single row and would understate real exposure the moment price has run past the trigger.
    To buy level
    How far today's price is from the buy trigger. “through” means price is already past it — good news, not an error.
    Box height
    (ceiling − floor) / floor. The height of the range itself — not a distance from your buy level. Measured on a real sweep, the buy level sits above the ceiling on 91% of candidates but inside the box on the other 9%, so there is no relationship between the two to assume.
    R = …
    The per-share risk the method itself defines: buy level minus stop-loss. 1R/2R/3R are that distance measured upward. Not a target — see the note in section 3 on why Darvas never gave one. An “already +N.R” marker means price has covered that many multiples of the risk before you would have paid anything for it.
    ₹cr/day
    See section 9.
    50 EMA / 100 EMA
    The 50 and 100-session exponential moving average of the close. Not a DAR-CARD rule — the deck's only EMA usage is a stop-ladder exit rule (5/10/20/200), not a trend filter. This is a trend-context overlay added on top of the classification, the same footing as liquidity and box height: informational, filterable, and never part of the tier or action itself. On the Advisor and Levels views it also shows as a small trend badge beside the action chip — omitted entirely when there is not yet enough history to read it, rather than guessed.

    6. The three views

    Advisor
    “What do I do today?” in urgency order: sell first, then hold, then buy candidates. Best for a quick daily check.
    Levels
    A to-scale price ladder per instrument — floor, ceiling, buy level, stop-loss, now — because Darvas is a visual method and neither other view draws the picture. Best for studying one or a handful of candidates.
    Table
    Every instrument, every field, sortable and filterable, nothing summarised. Best when you want the whole universe in front of you at once.

    7. The filters

    Stop-loss placement
    Whether the stop-loss lands above or below the level price broke out from. Above it, a stop-out still leaves part of the breakout move; below it, a stop-out gives the whole move back. On a real sweep, 57% of candidates carry a stop-loss below their breakout level.
    Liquidity
    A floor on median traded value — see section 9.
    Box height
    A tight box keeps the floor — rule C's exit — closer to the buy level. Not a claim that tight is safer; DarvaX makes no safety claim about a method labelled experimental and unvalidated.
    Trend
    Whether today's price sits above or below both the 50 and 100-session EMA. Not one of Darvas' own rules — a trend-context overlay the owner asked to add. A row where the two EMAs disagree, or where either is unmeasured, matches neither option rather than being guessed into one.

    8. The stop-loss

    The deck contradicts itself on stop sizing: Darvas' own canonical rule is 10% below entry on the first breakout (p.67); DarvaX's own “How to Play” section elsewhere in the same deck says 1% (p.44). A 1% stop on a breakout entry is removed by ordinary noise, so these are not two versions of one system. DarvaX defaults to the canonical, attributable 10% rule and treats the 1% variant as configuration, not as a second recommendation.

    For a recorded holding, the stop-loss is computed once, when you add the position, and frozen from that point on: if the active policy is later changed, an already-open position keeps the stop it was actually protected by rather than being silently re-priced.

    9. Liquidity

    DarvaX has no market-capitalisation data anywhere in ATHENA, so it measures traded value instead: the median of (close × volume) over the last 20 sessions, shown in ₹ crore per day. Median, not mean, so one 500× volume spike cannot flatter an otherwise thin stock. An instrument with fewer than 10 of those 20 sessions available reports as unmeasured — a gap in DarvaX's own data, never shown as a claim that the instrument is illiquid.

    10. What gets screened

    A sweep screens the darvax_discovery universe (ADR-011), ATHENA's own resolved instrument universe, read as data through DarvaX's existing read-only port. DarvaX imports no ATHENA resolver directly — it never asks ATHENA a question, it only reads what ATHENA has already published.

    11. Validated, or not?

    Every DarvaX output carries the status EXPERIMENTAL_UNVALIDATED because the source deck ships no backtest evidence of any kind. DarvaX runs its own outcome harness against ATHENA's real history, but the result only becomes VALIDATED once it clears a sufficiency gate: at least 200 closed trades and at least 500 trading days of coverage. Short of either bar, a measured expectancy is not evidence — it is a coin flip that has not been tossed enough times to mean anything, so the label stays honest about that rather than dressing up an insufficient sample as a result.

    12. What DarvaX will not do

    • It will not give you a profit target. The method has none; see section 3.
    • It will not produce a single blended score. Eligibility is a classification onto Darvas' own four rules, never a 0–100 index — that would manufacture precision the method cannot support.
    • It will not filter on market capitalisation. ATHENA holds no such data; liquidity is the closest measured substitute and is labelled as such.
    • It will not place an order, ever, under any configuration.
    • It will not let a config change silently reprice a position you already hold — see section 8.

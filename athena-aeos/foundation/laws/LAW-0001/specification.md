# Purpose

This document defines the immutable engineering laws governing every aspect of AEOS.

Violation of these laws constitutes an architectural defect.

---

# LAW-01 — Specification Before Implementation

Every implementation SHALL originate from an approved specification.

---

# LAW-02 — Architecture Before Code

Architecture defines implementation.

Implementation shall never define architecture.

---

# LAW-03 — Knowledge Is The Primary Asset

Engineering knowledge SHALL be preserved independently of implementation.

Code, documentation, tests, and workflows are derived artifacts.

---

# LAW-04 — Human Accountability

AI may recommend.

Humans approve.

Responsibility always remains with human engineers.

---

# LAW-05 — Governance Before Automation

Automation SHALL operate only within approved governance boundaries.

---

# LAW-06 — Vendor Neutrality

AEOS SHALL remain independent of:

- AI vendors
- IDEs
- Programming languages
- Frameworks
- Cloud providers

---

# LAW-07 — Separation of Concerns

Responsibilities SHALL remain independent.

Composition SHALL be preferred over coupling.

---

# LAW-08 — Traceability

Every engineering decision SHALL be traceable.

Origin, rationale, approvals and implementation history must remain discoverable.

---

# LAW-09 — Reusability

Reusable engineering knowledge SHALL always be preferred over duplication.

---

# LAW-10 — Explainability

Every engineering artifact SHALL be understandable by humans and autonomous systems.

---

# LAW-11 — Continuous Evolution

Every completed engineering activity SHALL improve future engineering work.

---

# LAW-12 — Backward Compatibility

Breaking changes SHALL be:

- Intentional
- Reviewed
- Versioned
- Documented

---

# LAW-13 — Security By Design

Security SHALL be considered from the beginning of every engineering lifecycle.

---

# LAW-14 — Quality Is Non-Negotiable

Testing, validation, review and verification are mandatory engineering activities.

---

# LAW-15 — Simplicity Over Complexity

The simplest solution satisfying architectural requirements SHALL always be preferred.

---
